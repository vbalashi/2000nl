# Learning vocabulary

## Meaning

One particular sense of a word. Two meanings with the same spelling are different
learning material. Knowing one does not assert knowledge of the others.

## Direction

The question and the answer for a meaning. Ordinary recall asks either for a
meaning from a word or for a word from a meaning. Knowing one direction does not
necessarily mean knowing the other.

## Known

The learner's explicit statement that they already know an answer and do not want
to practise that direction in ordinary training. This is independent of a review
grade or an estimate of how long the answer will remain in memory. The statement
applies across collections containing that same meaning.

## Knowledge choice

A proposed selection of the directions a learner considers known. The learner
can revise or abandon this choice before applying it.

## Return to learning

Withdraw a manual statement of knowledge so the direction can be practised again.
Prior learning history remains meaningful and is not erased by this choice.

## Previous meaning exclusion

A historical decision to stop practising both ordinary directions of one meaning.
The accepted interpretation is that the learner knew both answers. Historical
records retain the identity and scope of the original decision.
